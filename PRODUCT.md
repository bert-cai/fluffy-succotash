# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Small business owners affected by pending federal regulations. They are non-experts with no legal background, motivated because a proposed rule could hit their livelihood, but time-poor and easily intimidated. Their context: they've heard a regulation is coming, they suspect it matters to them, and they have a narrow window to act. They are not lawyers, lobbyists, or policy people. They need to understand the stakes fast and produce something credible without learning administrative law.

## Product Purpose

Civly finds federal regulations open for public comment and helps an ordinary person file a substantive comment the agency is legally obligated to address. It exists because the rulemaking process is technically open to everyone but practically dominated by corporate lobbyists who can afford counsel; Regulations.gov is free but impenetrable. Civly closes that gap: it surfaces the rules that matter (sorted by urgency, filterable by agency), explains the impact in plain language, runs an AI interview to draw out the user's specific situation, and drafts a detailed comment. Data comes from Regulations.gov, refreshed every 6 hours. Success is one thing: a first-time visitor submits a real, substantive comment on a rule that affects them.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy and plain-spoken. Civic, credible, and calm, like a public-interest tool that respects your time and tells it straight. Three words: **credible, plain-spoken, civic.** The voice never shouts and never condescends; it explains a serious process clearly enough that a busy non-expert feels capable and safe acting on it. Warmth comes from the human, on-your-side framing, not from decoration. It should feel like something built by people who take both the user and the institution seriously.

## Anti-references

- **Government / bureaucratic portals** (Regulations.gov, federal form sites): cold, dense, intimidating, form-heavy. This is precisely the experience Civly replaces; never inherit its tone or layout.
- **Generic SaaS templates**: gradient hero, identical icon-card grids, buzzword marketing, the interchangeable startup look. Civly is a civic tool, not a Series A pitch.
- **Cheap or untrustworthy surfaces**: anything that reads like a scam, a data-harvesting funnel, or a low-effort side project. Civly handles something consequential (a legally binding public record), and the craft must signal that it can be trusted.

Also avoid a partisan / activist register: Civly is nonpartisan and earns trust by being credible, not by being loud.

## Design Principles

- **Lower the activation energy.** Every screen should make the next action obvious and small. The user's hardest moment is starting; design removes friction, never adds ceremony.
- **Plain language is the interface.** Explain the stakes and the process in words a busy non-expert understands. Clarity is the product; jargon is the bureaucracy we're replacing.
- **Earn trust through craft and candor.** Polish, accurate deadlines, honest framing, and visible sourcing (Regulations.gov) signal legitimacy. Never overstate; the truth (your comment is legally binding) is already strong.
- **Urgency without alarm.** Comment windows close. Surface time pressure honestly and calmly so people act in time, without manufacturing anxiety.
- **The comment is the point.** Browse, filter, and interview all exist to funnel toward one filed, substantive comment. Cut anything that doesn't move the user toward submitting.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Body text holds ≥4.5:1 contrast (watch the muted `--color-mid` gray on the cream `--color-light` bg, it is the likeliest failure and should be reserved for large or non-essential text). Full keyboard operability across browse, filters, and the interview flow. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` on every animation. Semantic markup and screen-reader-friendly labels throughout. Given a broad, non-technical public audience, favor generous tap targets, legible type sizes, and plain-language errors over cleverness.
